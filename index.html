Archive guide

Methodology & coverage

What counts as a warning?

Each initial operational FF.W warning counts once per issuing office, VTEC year and event number. The original warning segment must explicitly link the threat to burned terrain. The screen excludes follow-up issuances, dam or levee failures and generic safety advice. Each event links to its IEM warning record.

What does the map show?

Roll the mouse wheel over the map to zoom toward the pointer, or use the + and − buttons. Polygons show the initial NWS warning footprint. The legend lets you color them by issuance season or meteorology named in the warning. A burn-scar perimeter may cover a different area. If the initial IEM polygon is unavailable, the tracker uses coordinates printed in that warning. A text-verified warning with no usable shape still contributes to its office total.

How far back can I compare offices?

The search begins November 1, 2005. Some early warnings mention burned areas but lack an operational NEW tag. Storm-based warnings became official October 1, 2007. Early counts and comparisons over time reflect changes in tagging, coverage and warning language.

How are seasons and names assigned?

Peak season is the three consecutive calendar months with the most archived warnings for an office. Ties share the peak. This describes issuance timing. Monthly and annual cells cover the full archive from November 1, 2005 through the latest successful IEM check. Zero means that the search found no qualifying initial warning for that period. Burn names come from the warning text and some remain unspecified. Heatmap shares use each row’s full archive total. The heat map gives regional weather context for the selected month. Annual cells identify the busiest month or months in that year. Each populated cell links to its latest IEM warning record. The weather filter applies to archived warnings. Each live NWS warning remains visible in its selected region.

How is the meteorology identified?

The tracker searches the initial warning segment for explicit mentions of thunderstorms, monsoon, an atmospheric river, a front, rainbands or a tropical cyclone. Open a polygon or a recent event to read the supporting wording. A warning can match several terms and counts once in the archive. “No tracked setup named” means the segment did not identify any of these setups. Short-fuse warnings often describe local rainfall without naming its larger-scale cause. Establishing that cause requires additional weather data.

How current is the tracker?

The scheduled archive job checks the previous seven days every 30 minutes. Active NWS alerts are checked every two minutes while the page is open. The live indicator shows the last successful check or an unavailable status.
