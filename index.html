Archive guide

Methodology & coverage

What counts as a warning?

Each initial operational FF.W warning counts once per issuing office, VTEC year and event number. The original warning segment must explicitly link the threat to burned terrain. The screen excludes follow-up issuances, dam or levee failures and generic safety advice. Each event links to its IEM warning record.

What does the map show?

Amber shapes show the initial NWS warning footprint. A burn-scar perimeter may cover a different area. If the initial IEM polygon is unavailable, the tracker uses coordinates printed in that warning. A text-verified warning with no usable shape still contributes to its office total.

How far back can I compare offices?

The search begins November 1, 2005. Some early warnings mention burned areas but lack an operational NEW tag. Storm-based warnings became official October 1, 2007. Early counts and comparisons over time reflect changes in tagging, coverage and warning language.

How are seasons and names assigned?

Peak season is the three consecutive calendar months with the most archived warnings for an office. Ties share the peak. This describes issuance timing. Monthly cells sum all archive years; annual cells start in 2015 and the current year is partial. Burn names come from the warning text and some remain unspecified.

How current is the tracker?

The scheduled archive job checks the previous seven days every 30 minutes. Active NWS alerts are checked every two minutes while the page is open. The live indicator shows the last successful check or an unavailable status.
